# Mechanical Engineering Reference

## Three Phase Induction Motor

### Working Principle
A three-phase induction motor converts electrical energy to mechanical energy via electromagnetic induction. The rotating magnetic field of the stator induces current in the rotor, creating torque.

### Key Components

| Component | Function | Failure Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Stator | Creates rotating magnetic field | Overheating, winding fault |
| Rotor | Converts EM induction to rotation | Bar breakage, eccentricity |
| Bearings | Support shaft rotation | Wear, fatigue, lubrication failure |
| Cooling Fan | Dissipates heat | Blade damage, blockage |
| Shaft | Transmits mechanical power | Misalignment, unbalance |
| End Shields | House bearings | Loosening |

### Normal Operating Parameters

| Parameter | Typical Value | Warning | Critical |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature (winding) | 40–75°C | >75°C | >90°C |
| Vibration | <2.8 mm/s | >5.0 mm/s | >8.0 mm/s |
| Current (full load) | 80–100% FLA | >110% FLA | >130% FLA |
| RPM (2-pole, 50Hz) | ~2,950 rpm | <2,700 rpm | <2,400 rpm |

### ISO Vibration Standards
Per ISO 10816-3 (machines 15kW–300kW):
- Zone A (new): < 2.3 mm/s RMS
- Zone B (acceptable): 2.3–4.5 mm/s RMS
- Zone C (alarm): 4.5–7.1 mm/s RMS
- Zone D (danger): > 7.1 mm/s RMS
